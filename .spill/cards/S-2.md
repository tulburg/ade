## Purpose
Prove the ADE illustration language before anything else is built. The look of the illustration decides the direction of the format, the viewer and the generator, so it is drawn and approved here first.

The POC uses one real topic end to end: **How an AI coding agent works** (main concept), with prerequisite levels such as **L1 What a language model does**.

ADE reads like a dictionary or thesaurus: a reader at any level should understand everything after opening the document, so every complex word or concept on the page is explained.

## Requirements
1. Full-screen reader page, like a PDF viewer: no app name, no toolbar, no list of levels. One level is shown at a time.
2. Each prerequisite level opens with a cover: its question alone, centred and full screen, Yes and No under it. Yes slides the next page in from the right; No reveals the level.
3. The cover's answer stands: a revealed page asks nothing more. It shows the title, the explanation paragraph and the figure, with a Next button at the bottom right. At most 3 levels (up to 2 prerequisites + main).
4. The explanation is a full paragraph of about 5 lines, justified. Complex words and concepts in it are underlined; clicking one opens its full definition under the word (what it means, where it is in the figure, its source). Claims carry numbered references ([1]) that open their source.
5. The layout is justified: the paragraph and the figure span the page from the left margin to the right margin.
6. The only persistent indicator is the main concept (bottom left), so the reader knows which document they opened.
7. Figures are flat 2D drawings (front view, no isometric or 3D) in the anatomy style: monochrome thin inks, engraved name plates, small precise parts. No frame or corner text around the figure.
8. Every part that needs explaining is clickable and carries a blue reference tag (e.g. `C2`) in its top-left or top-right corner, outside the part only when it is too small. Tags are how explainers cite parts, across levels too (`L1 A2`). The blue outline shows only on hover or when open.
9. Each clickable part has a brief caption (5 to 8 words) inside it next to its tag, in a soft text colour; each connection has a tiny italic note.
10. Opening a part shows its explainer as a popup on the part, with an arrow pointing at it: what it is, how it connects (with tags), and sources (links, PDFs).
11. At most 3 moving parts per figure; they are the only red and carry a numbered marker.
12. Light theme first, dark theme defined.

## Out of scope
- The document format / bundle, the generator, sharing, and working code (motion and clicks are described, not built). This spec is the design only.

## Acceptance checks
- Artboards show: the centred cover, the Yes slide, a revealed prerequisite (closed, and with a term definition open), the main concept with a part explainer open, the marks/motion/rules sheet, and the dark theme.
- Every clickable part has a tag; no figure has more than 3 moving parts.
- The user approves the design on the board.
